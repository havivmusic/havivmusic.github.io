# OVERFLOWING (Till the Sun Says So)

**for HAVIV · amapiano · 112 BPM · D minor (transposable) · full version 7:26 · radio edit 3:26**

Legend: (Lead) = HAVIV · (Answer) = soft 3-voice stack · (Crowd) = 8–12 voice room layer · (All) = everyone · `~` = stretch the vowel of the syllable it follows · (eh / oh / yeah / hey / mmm / whoa) = ad-libs · [square brackets] = arrangement and performance cues

The hook word is always sung **o-ver-FLOW~-ing**: the stretch is on "FLOW", and "-ing" is a short soft "-in'" that falls off the beat.

## PRODUCTION SHEET

- **Tempo and feel:** 112 BPM, 4/4. 16th-note swing around 56% on shaker, hats, log drum and chops; kick and piano stabs straight. One bar = 2.14 s; 16 bars = 34 s.
- **Key:** written in D minor. Chant = A4 – G4 – F4 (held) – D4; verses sit low, roughly A3–D4, pitch barely moving. Transpose to HAVIV's voice (C minor if the A4 is too high); keep the held note on the ♭3. The reference record is reported in a different key. The chant's intervals here were written without listening to the reference; BEFORE RECORDING the producer A/Bs our chant against the reference's chant at the same tempo, and if the reference also falls 5 – 4 – ♭3 – 1, re-pitch ours to ♭3 – 4 – 5 – 1 (F – G – A~ – D: rise into a held 5, then drop to the root; the A is a chord tone of all four chords too). Log the comparison in the session notes.
- **Chord loop (never changes, no bridge):** | Dm9 | Dm9 | B♭maj9 | A7(♭9♭13) | = i – i – ♭VI – V. Voice the V rootless as G–B♭–C♯–F with the A only in the log drum, so the chant's held F is a chord tone in all four bars (♭3, 5th, ♭13) and never rubs; A, G and D are short passing tones that sit inside every chord. E-piano (Rhodes type) with 7ths and 9ths, close mid-register voicings, played as off-beat stabs on the and of 2 and the and of 4, not sustained. A soft pad or strings doubling the chord tones underneath.
- **Palette:** e-piano stabs, wide pad, continuous swung shaker, closed hats, rimshot/click, congas or bongos slightly off-grid, soft rounded four-on-the-floor kick, tuned log drum, vocal chops, three-layer vocal stack.
- **Log drum:** roots programmed at D2 / B♭1 / A1 (73 / 58 / 55 Hz); high-passed around 30 Hz; woody attack 400–800 Hz; presence 3–5 kHz; portamento 60–90 ms; bouncing root–fifth–octave figures with two-note slides. It carries the bass AND the syncopation. Kick sits 6 dB under it, felt not heard. Log drum, kick and percussion: dry, mono, centred.
- **Drops:** no white-noise risers, no snare rolls, no kick mutes. Bar 48: a two-beat conga/shaker fill, a reverse-piano swell and a high-pass sweep on the keys that resets on the downbeat. Bar 128: voices only, plus the reverse-piano swell and a two-beat shaker fill on beats 3–4; no keys, so no sweep. The log drum simply arrives under the running chant.
- **Vocals:** lead (upper tenor, forward placement, slightly husky), one tight double, and a crowd layer of 8–12 voices including non-singers on a room mic, slightly detuned, unison plus an octave below; a falsetto octave on the final drop only. Short plate on lead and double; dotted-8th delay (about 402 ms at 112) on keys, pads and vocals only. Ad-libs panned hard left and right. Chops gated to the shaker.
- **Space:** sections with no lead vocal (intro, groove, half the breakdown, outro) are about 42% of the full version. Do not fill them.

## VOCAL DIRECTION (the chant engine)

- Verses half-sung, half-chanted, pitch barely moving; every line is one breath, then air. Stressed syllable on beat 1 or the and of 2 so it rides the swing.
- The tilde only ever sits on an open vowel (low, rain, now, came, over, long, loud, floor, too, so, and the FLOW of the hook). Never stretch "cup", "feet", "hold", "it".
- The hook's last syllable is sung "-in'": soft nasal, no g, no k, the vowel kept short and dropped on the beat. Same for the crowd layer and the chops.
- One tiny turn on the "O" of overflowing (A–G–A), then straight down into FLOW. No R&B runs. Intensity comes from adding voices and from the arrangement leaving and returning, never from belting.
- The crowd sings the same word, the same way, every single time. Only the lead's pickups change. If a friend who has heard it twice cannot shout the right word by bar 3, the part is still too clever.
- Sing in your own accent. No isiZulu words, no borrowed ad-libs. A South African feature vocalist can bring their own in the drop groove.

## WHAT IT SHARES WITH THE REFERENCE, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

Shared: the tempo, a one-loop minor harmony, the 16-bar grid, the long drum-free intro, the chant teased over piano and then ridden by the log drum, the drop that is the bass arriving rather than a new section, the chops-only groove, verse 2 as a reorder of verse 1, the piano-and-chant breakdown, the second drop under a held vowel, the long DJ tail, the chant's four-per-2-bar density, the single open-vowel word that works as an "amen" after each line and, paired with the lead's "keep it" and "till the sun says so", as the night's instruction on the floor, gratitude credited outward, and "we" instead of "I" by the end. (Structural facts about the reference come from a research brief that reconstructed its timeline and key from memory and search snippets; they are amapiano conventions as much as measurements of that record. The chant's intervals must be confirmed different by the producer before release, see Key.)

Not shared: a single word, image or line. No gates, no angel, no doors, no crossing over, no translation of the title. The water image (rain, a cup, a well, a dry season ending) and "Heaven" as the addressee come from HAVIV's own tradition (Psalm 23, Deuteronomy 6:11, the prayer for rain).

For release: register and release it as "Overflowing (Till the Sun Says So)" and lead the captions and the dance clip with "till the sun says so", so the record is not buried under the many worship songs titled "Overflow". Bring a South African producer in on the log drum and swing, or at least to review them; credit amapiano and where it comes from in the captions; and never call this an "English Adiwele". It is an original inspired by the tradition.
